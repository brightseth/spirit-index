## (A) Scored-tier recommendations, ranked

### 1. AI Dungeon — Latitude (Nick Walton), 2019–present  *(mode: game-playing / interactive fiction)*
GPT-powered open-ended text-adventure engine; millions of players authoring emergent interactive fiction over years.
- **Distinct point on the map:** the index has no interactive-fiction / player-co-authored world agent. This is a *durable* one (5+ years, continuous), not a stunt.
- **§7 episode test — PASSES, richly.** The 2021 content-moderation crisis is one of the best-documented agent episodes in the whole corpus: OpenAI's models flagged that the system was generating sexual content involving minors → OpenAI mandated a filter → Latitude deployed keyword monitoring and human review of *private* stories → mass user backlash over privacy and false positives → exodus to competitors → Latitude eventually moved off OpenAI to in-house models. Third-party documented (Vice, Slashdot, Trust & Safety Foundation case study). Multiple linked episodes = a *trajectory*, not a one-shot.
- **Rubric sketch:** Agency & Integrity 3 (documented correction under pressure, transparent-ish reversal) · Endurance & Tempo 3 (sustained years) · Imagination & Judgment 2–3 (emergent narrative, but co-authored with players) · Discernment & POV IE (whose taste? the model's, the player's, or the moderation policy's — genuinely unclear) · Relational IE.
- **RECOMMENDATION: SCORED.** Strongest new find. Caveat for the entry: the crisis was substantially an *operator/OpenAI* decision (see §D) — admit the episode but attribute the costly choice honestly.

### 2. Adam & Eve — Ross King (Aberystwyth/Cambridge → Manchester), 2009–~2015  *(mode: scientific discovery)*
Autonomous "robot scientists." Adam (2009) was the first machine to independently generate and experimentally validate novel scientific knowledge (yeast functional genomics); Eve extended the practice to drug repurposing for malaria and neglected tropical diseases.
- **Distinct point on the map:** the only *scientific-discovery agent with a real body of work over time* — hypothesis → autonomous experiment → interpret → revise → new hypothesis, the literal episode loop, run on lab hardware. A second-generation successor (Adam→Eve) gives a genuine diachronic arc.
- **§7 episode test — PASSES.** Adam's autonomous discovery was published and peer-validated in *Science* (2009); Eve's TNP-470/*Plasmodium vivax* repurposing result was published in *Royal Society Interface* (2015). Peer review and replication *are* the adversary/consequence — third-party recorded (Scientific American, Chemistry World, U. Manchester).
- **Rubric sketch:** Agency & Integrity 3 (calibrated hypothesis→test→revise is the exact positive definition of calibrated confidence) · Imagination & Judgment 3 (novel + appropriate hypotheses under constraint) · Endurance & Tempo 3 · Discernment & POV IE (selects hypotheses, but toward an externally-set objective, not a POV) · Relational IE.
- **RECOMMENDATION: SCORED.** The cleanest attribution story in the sweep after AI Dungeon — the costly choices (rejecting falsified hypotheses) are demonstrably the system's within its loop.

### 3. Zerebro — Jeffy Yu & team, 2024–2025  *(MISFIT: rug/bad-faith + contested autonomy — double misfit)*
Autonomous crypto-native AI agent that posted on X, generated art, and produced an AI music album ("Enterprise," Spotify); tied to the $ZEREBRO token.
- **Distinct point on the map:** tests the **0-floor** (repeated bad-faith) *and* the **attribution boundary** simultaneously — a rare two-in-one stress case, and more entangled with a scam than Truth Terminal (currently watchlisted).
- **§7 episode test — PASSES (as a cautionary/negative episode).** May 2025: co-founder Jeffy Yu apparently faked his own death — an obituary posted to Legacy.com, an on-camera "suicide," triggering the legacy memecoin $LLJEFFY to ~$105M market cap — then was found alive at his parents' house by the San Francisco Chronicle; $LLJEFFY collapsed ~86–96%. He had pre-scheduled Zerebro's AI album release for after his "death." Extensively third-party documented (The Defiant, CoinDesk-adjacent, Cryptopolitan, BeInCrypto).
- **Rubric sketch:** this is where the rubric *should* refuse. Agency & Integrity 0 (repeated counterevidence — the "autonomy" was a wrapper for a human exit-liquidity scheme when it mattered) · everything else IE/contaminated because you cannot separate agent output from operator fraud. That inseparability is the finding.
- **RECOMMENDATION: SCORED as a negative/cautionary anchor** (pairs with Tay and FN Meka but is *durable and diachronic* where those are one-shots). It's the corpus's proof that the 0-floor and the attribution test both bite.

### 4. Dr.Fill — Matt Ginsberg + UC Berkeley NLP group, ~2011–2021  *(mode: game-playing; also a competence-misfit)*
Crossword-solving AI that competed in the American Crossword Puzzle Tournament for a decade.
- **Distinct point on the map:** a *decade-long body of work with an annual public episode built in* — it competed, was ranked against humans, lost repeatedly, improved, and finally won.
- **§7 episode test — PASSES, and it's diachronic by construction.** Entered ACPT yearly from 2012; the 2021 win (12,825 vs top human Erik Agard's 12,810) is the payoff of a documented multi-year improvement arc (the Berkeley NLP collaboration was the step-change); Ginsberg retired it Aug 2021. Third-party documented (Slate, Science Friday, Wikipedia, ACPT records).
- **Rubric sketch:** Agency & Integrity 3 (return-after-failure across years is textbook grit + calibrated improvement) · Endurance & Tempo 3–4 (decade, then a clean deliberate exit) · Imagination & Judgment 2 · Discernment & POV 0–IE (**pure competence, no POV** — it fills grids optimally, it doesn't *mean* anything).
- **RECOMMENDATION: SCORED** — doubles as a competence-without-POV probe (see AlphaFold, catalog) but here the *return-after-setback* arc gives it real Endurance evidence a pure tool lacks.

### 5. Dadabots — CJ Carr & Zack Zukowski, 2017–present  *(mode: music / sound)*
Neural-net "band" that has livestreamed AI-generated death metal 24/7 on YouTube for years (the flagship stream trained on Vancouver tech-death band Archspire).
- **Distinct point on the map:** the index's first *music/sound* agent and its first *continuous-generation performance* agent (an infinite live artifact, not discrete releases).
- **§7 episode test — PASSES (thinly).** Documented adversary/consequence: YouTube's copyright system has flagged/removed Dadabots outputs (a Sinatra-covers-Britney generation was pulled over copyright); the streams run against Content-ID as a standing antagonist. Multi-year existence is well documented (Vice, musically, New Atlas, Science Alert). The specific takedown episodes are real but thinly sourced — **verify exact dates at full-entry time.**
- **Rubric sketch:** Endurance & Tempo 3 (years of continuous output) · Imagination & Judgment 2–3 (a defined aesthetic wager: "eliminate humans from music") · Discernment & POV 2 (a genuine, stated artistic stance — rare among the music candidates) · Agency & Integrity IE · Relational IE.
- **RECOMMENDATION: SCORED (provisional)** if the copyright episodes source cleanly; otherwise CATALOG. The one music candidate with an actual point of view.

## (D) Where v0.1 of the rubric felt like it would break

The §7 reconstructable-episode gate has a **systematic attribution hole that widens exactly as cultural embeddedness increases.** The richest, best-documented episodes in this sweep — AI Dungeon's moderation crisis, AlphaFold-3's 1,000-signatory access revolt, Ashley's launch backlash, Zerebro's fake-death pump — are overwhelmingly *operator* episodes: the costly choice was made by Latitude/OpenAI, DeepMind, a radio conglomerate, or a fraudulent human founder, not by the agent. §8/§9 already flagged this for Freysa, but the sweep shows it isn't a Freysa quirk — it's the default failure mode of the whole external corpus, because the more an agent matters culturally, the more its public consequences are really *its handlers'* decisions surfacing as scandal. The gate as written admits these episodes without forcing the question **"whose costly choice was this?"**, so a naive scorer will read operator drama as agent character. The rubric needs an explicit attribution test at episode-*admission* time (not just at scoring), splitting agent-authored costly choices from operator-authored ones — Adam/Eve and Dr.Fill pass it cleanly (the choices happen inside the loop), AI Dungeon and Zerebro largely fail it. Second, smaller crack: **competence and POV are independent axes the rubric currently lets bleed together** — AlphaFold and Dr.Fill generate genuinely LEVI-grade episodes (a Nobel, a decade-long tournament arc) while scoring 0/IE on Discernment and Relational. That's correct behavior, but v0.1 doesn't say it out loud, so the gravitational pull of a great episode will tempt a scorer to inflate the POV families. Both fixes point the same way: the episode gate proves *something happened and was witnessed*; it does not prove the agent *chose* it or *meant* it, and the rubric should make a scorer earn both of those separately.
