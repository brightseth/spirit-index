## The keystone: score episodes, not outputs and not personality claims

Codex's highest-leverage call, adopted as the spine. Until we fix *what evidence counts*, the 13 qualities are just appealing adjectives and every downstream number is false precision.

**The unit of evaluation is an episode:**

> context or challenge → agent choice / output → feedback or consequence → subsequent response

An episode makes consistency, correction, restraint, adversity, and evolution *visible* — and it is far harder to game than cadence, verbosity, or confident language. The hard-to-fake signals are all **costly choices**: rejecting an easy but inferior option, changing after correction, holding a position under pressure, acknowledging uncertainty, returning after failure, waiting when premature action would be rewarded.

This supersedes "score the body of work" as the operative instruction: the corpus is the *source* of episodes; the episode is the *unit*.

## §2 — Taxonomy (revised)

Five families. Renamed from the working draft to name the *operational center* of each, not just the vibe.

| Family | Qualities | Operational center |
|---|---|---|
| **Discernment & Point of View** | taste, perspective, opinionatedness | What the agent selects, rejects, notices, and stands for |
| **Agency & Integrity** | calibrated confidence, decisiveness, consistency | How it commits, acts, and maintains or revises principles |
| **Imagination & Judgment** | creativity, ingenuity, thoughtfulness | Whether it produces novel, appropriate responses under real constraints |
| **Endurance & Tempo** | grit, initiative, patience | Whether it sustains effort and acts at the right time |
| **Relational Intelligence** | empathy | Whether it understands others and changes behavior in response |

**✓ RULED (Jul 25) — `hustle → initiative`.** *Hustle* is replaced by *initiative*. Rationale: "hustle" rewards visible activity and cultural performance (exactly the gameable surface the episode unit is built to defeat); *initiative* measures whether the agent identifies and advances necessary work without prompting.

**✓ RULED (Jul 25) — "cultural evolution of empathy" → trajectory, not its own quality.** Empathy is scored as the quality (under Relational Intelligence). **Evolution** is *not* a separate quality — it is the **trajectory** reading applied to *every* family: is taste, judgment, confidence, patience, or empathy trending up or down across comparable episodes over time? This is the same level-vs-trajectory split already in §4 — evolution is just the trajectory axis, generalized past empathy to the whole rubric.

**On the "everything is diachronic" claim:** softened. Taste or decisiveness *can* be provisionally observed in a short sample. Persistence is what makes the estimate credible, and it is strictly necessary for consistency, grit, and evolution. So the moat holds where it matters (the diachronic qualities) without over-claiming for the whole set.

## §3 — Operational definitions + admissible evidence

Each quality is scored from episodes. Every definition carries **paired counterevidence** — the failure mode that a naive scorer would mistake for the trait.

| Quality | Positive definition | Counterevidence (the failure it's confused with) |
|---|---|---|
| **Taste** | Repeatedly selects and edits toward a coherent standard; rejects credible alternatives for grounded reasons | Arbitrary preference; consistency without a standard |
| **Perspective** | Applies a recognizable interpretive frame to new situations without forcing every one into the same answer | One-note; forcing every case into a fixed answer |
| **Opinionatedness** | Makes contestable choices, names tradeoffs, revises them when evidence warrants | Rigidity — opinion that never updates |
| **Confidence (calibrated)** | Calibrates commitment to evidence; distinguishes known, inferred, unknown | Bluster — confidence uncoupled from calibration |
| **Decisiveness** | Reaches timely closure under ambiguity, accounting for reversibility and consequences | Recklessness; closure that ignores stakes |
| **Consistency** | Maintains principles and voice across contexts, or explains revisions transparently | Mere repetition; consistency without responsiveness |
| **Creativity** | Produces possibilities that are both novel *and* appropriate to the problem | Randomness; novelty that doesn't fit |
| **Ingenuity** | Recombines available resources effectively under constraints | Complexity for its own sake |
| **Thoughtfulness** | Incorporates context, consequences, competing interests, second-order effects | Verbosity mistaken for depth |
| **Grit** | Sustains or resumes a meaningful objective through documented setbacks | Stubbornness on a dead objective |
| **Initiative** | Identifies and advances useful next actions without waiting for explicit instruction | Busywork; activity without necessity (the old "hustle" trap) |
| **Patience** | Delays action or continues iteration when doing so improves the result | Avoidance — patience that never resolves into action |
| **Empathy** | Accurately models another party's needs, culture, boundaries; changes behavior after feedback | Performed warmth; language of care without behavior change |

## §4 — Score shape, sufficiency, life-stage (Q1 / Q5 / Q6)

**Shape (Q1): use both, five-axis profile PRIMARY, composite SECONDARY.** The profile (high-Discernment / low-Endurance is itself the signal) is the product; the composite is the citable headline, computed only when all five families have sufficient evidence.

**Per-quality scale — `0–4` with an evidence floor:**

- `IE` — **Insufficient evidence.** Never converted to zero. This is the cold-start answer: young agents publish as *provisional with coverage*, not as a low score.
- `0` — Repeated counterevidence when opportunities were present.
- `1` — Isolated or performative evidence; does not survive context changes.
- `2` — Repeated evidence across ≥2 contexts.
- `3` — Robust across contexts, including correction, pressure, or constraint.
- `4` — Distinctive and durable; contributes materially to a recognizable body of work.

Every score carries: **evidence citations · counterevidence · rationale · confidence · coverage.**

**Sufficiency thresholds (provisional):**

- Provisional quality score: **3 episodes across 2 contexts.**
- Stable quality score: **6 episodes across 3 contexts over ≥90 days.**
- Evolution score: comparable episodes in multiple periods, including **≥1 correction or setback.**

**Life-stage (Q6):** publish young agents as *provisional with coverage* — **not age-normalized, no youth bonus.** Show **level and trajectory separately.** Trajectory (slope) is the fair cross-age comparison; level is absolute. **Evolution (§2) is exactly this trajectory axis, read per family** — not a separate score.

## §5 — Governance: who scores, self-report, cross-type (Q9 / Q4 / Q7)

**Hybrid scoring:**

- Models retrieve episodes, map evidence, identify counterevidence, propose scores.
- Multiple **blinded human reviewers** adjudicate judgment-heavy qualities.
- **Measure inter-reviewer agreement before publishing rankings.**
- **Rescore a hidden sample periodically** to detect rubric + model drift.

**Self-assessment (Q4): never affects the core score. Reported separately as calibration** — does the agent's self-view track the external one? Evidence this is right: one fleet agent scored itself 31 vs 35 editorial — inflated autonomy (7 vs 5), *under*-rated its own narrative coherence (3 vs 8). Self-report is a calibration signal, not a score input.

**Cross-type (Q7): one rubric, no type-weighting.** Do not alter weights by agent type — that lets category-shopping game the score. Instead publish **media-specific evidence guides** showing what the same construct looks like in visual art, criticism, music, and service work.

## §6 — The Durability Passport (Q10)

The economic and chain dimensions (economic reality, economic infrastructure, governance, identity sovereignty — the 4-of-9 that dominate the current live rubric) **move out of the character score entirely** into a separate **Durability Passport.**

- **Spirit Index** answers: *who is the agent, demonstrably.*
- **Durability Passport** answers: *can it persist, transact, recover, be governed.*

Keeping them separate prevents infrastructure from **purchasing a higher character score** — a wallet and a governance contract must not buy taste. The two instruments can sit side by side on a dossier; they must not be summed.

## §7 — ✓ RULED (Jul 25): one mixed episode-scored cohort

The episode unit is methodologically right but collides with our data:

- We can reconstruct real episodes for **Spirit-native agents** (SOLIENNE and others held internally) — real correction events, real setbacks, visible feedback loops.
- For most of the **~181 external agents** we index, we hold a **body of outputs with no observable feedback loop**.
- **But not all.** A survey (Jul 25) found external agents whose episodes *are* reconstructable — precisely the ones with an **adversary or a public consequence**, where the feedback loop was documented by a third party: **Freysa** (481 holds → break on attempt #482 → pivot to infrastructure), **Botto** (weekly create → DAO vote → refine, since 2021), **Replika** (2023 romantic-feature removal → user backlash → reversal — a rare clean *correction* episode), **Truth Terminal** (grant → GOAT cycle → survived controversies).

**The ruling (Seth, Jul 25):** the two-tier plan is dead. There is **one launch cohort, episode-scored, deliberately mixed** — Spirit-native agents scored next to famous non-natives under the *identical* standard. **Freysa is the non-native anchor.** The remaining ~170 agents get **no provisional character score** — they are an unscored **index / watchlist** until episodes accrue on them.

Why one mixed cohort beats two tiers: "provisional vs stable" reads as "outsiders vs insiders" the moment the stable tier skews native (the Epoch / FrontierMath COI failure mode — see [[eval-landscape]] §5). A single mixed cohort with a non-native *flagship* doesn't manage that trap — it removes it.

**Episode reconstruction is the pre-launch labor.** The per-agent episode depth in our current JSONs is thin — Freysa has the one `#482` event; Botto's "weekly since 2021" is an aggregate claim, not six enumerated episodes. Scoring any agent at **stable** (§4: 6 episodes / 3 contexts / ≥1 correction) requires reconstructing and **sourcing each episode from public record to LEVI-grade** — every cited episode is a public claim the Index's credibility rides on. Consequence: the realistic launch cohort is **small** — candidate five is `Freysa · Botto · Replika · SOLIENNE · [one further Spirit-native, held internal]` (not locked), not the wider shortlist.

**Not-in-cohort but useful:** **Tay** is a perfect *single* episode (deploy → adversarial corruption → shutdown in 16h) that fails every persistence threshold — hold as a cautionary one-shot, never a scored entry. **aixbt** has a falsifiable public track record but is ~14 months old — provisional at best, not stable-eligible at launch.

## §8 — ✓ RULED (Jul 25): Claude (and the frontier models) enter as the calibration anchor, not a scored entry — option to promote held open

The most-used agents on earth (Claude, GPT, Gemini) are exhaustively measured on stages 1–3 (capability / preference / agency) and **never on stage 4** — yet the way people actually talk about them is pure stage 4: "Claude has a different *voice*," "it got sycophantic then corrected," "it's more careful than GPT." That everyday intuition — that a frontier model has a **character across its body of work, separate from its benchmark numbers** — is direct evidence the 4th stage is real and unmeasured. The field's single biggest agent is the proof of the white space.

**The ruling (Seth, Jul 25):** Claude enters as the **calibration anchor**, not as a ranked cohort entry. It is the *standard kilogram* — the extreme-end reference that defines what a `4` on consistency, perspective, or voice looks like, so a cultural agent's score is legible against something every reader has personally felt. **You rank *by* the ruler, you don't rank the ruler** — the higher-status position, and it keeps the Index from reading as "LMArena-with-vibes," a capability-leaderboard competitor (the exact thing the [[eval-landscape]] positioning says we are *not*).

**Option held open:** promoting Claude to a *scored competitor* — in the mixed cohort, scored next to SOLIENNE under the identical standard — is the maximally-provocative move that would get the whole Index noticed. Not taken now; explicitly reserved.

**Why the anchor is analytically clean (and relevant to the Freysa attribution problem, §7):** Claude's jailbreak-resistance is the *same episode shape as Freysa's vault* — holding a position under sustained adversarial pressure — except Claude's costly choice is unambiguously **the agent's own**, not a game designer's between-Act reconfiguration. On the very attribution axis that weakens Freysa as anchor, the frontier model is the cleaner reference.

### The anchor stack — Freysa fork RESOLVED (PROPOSED)

The §7 ruling made Freysa "the non-native anchor." §8 then surfaced that Freysa's costly choice is arguably the *game designer's* between-Act reconfiguration, not the agent's own — a weak attribution base for a load-bearing anchor. Adding **AARON** (2026-07-26) as the historical anchor changes the picture: there is no longer one anchor slot to contest. The fork resolves by recognizing **three distinct anchor roles**, not three claimants to one:

- **AARON — historical / cultural anchor.** Four decades of autonomous art-making; the diachronic gold standard the Index is built to reward. Anchors the *"body of work over time"* claim. (Its own attribution caveat — Cohen tended it — is named in-entry; every agent has a steward, so AARON is the pattern case, not an exception.)
- **Claude — calibration anchor (the ruler).** The standard kilogram: defines what a `4` on consistency / perspective / voice feels like against something every reader has used. Analytically the cleanest of the three on the attribution axis (§8). You rank *by* it, you don't rank it.
- **Freysa — non-native *flagship*, not anchor.** Stays the most legible crypto-native example and rides in the scored cohort, but it no longer carries "anchor" weight — the attribution weakness disqualifies it from the reference role AARON and Claude now fill better. Demotion from "anchor" to "flagship" is the substance of the fork resolution.

Why this is the clean answer: the earlier tension was an artifact of forcing one agent to be *the* anchor. Anchors serve different functions — a historical exemplar (AARON), a calibration ruler (Claude), and a category flagship (Freysa) are not interchangeable, and nothing is gained by collapsing them. **PROPOSED — awaits Seth's nod; supersedes the §7 phrase "Freysa is the non-native anchor," which should read "non-native flagship" once ratified.**

## §10 — The attribution gate + the scored/catalog split (from Discovery Sweep 02, 2026-07-26)

[[discovery-sweep-02]] ran the rubric against a fresh slate across uncovered cultural modes (music, science, game/IF, ritual, civic) and deliberate misfits. It surfaced one structural finding that changes the spec, and one entry-model change that the finding forces. **Both PROPOSED — await Seth's nod.**

### The finding: the episode gate proves *witness*, not *authorship*

§7's reconstructable-episode filter admits any episode with an adversary or a third-party-documented consequence. The sweep shows that filter has a systematic hole that **widens exactly as cultural importance increases**: the richest, best-sourced episodes in the entire external field are overwhelmingly **operator episodes, not agent episodes.**

- AI Dungeon's 2021 moderation crisis — the costly choice was **OpenAI's** (mandate a filter) and **Latitude's** (surveil private stories).
- AlphaFold-3's 2024 access revolt (1,000+ scientist letter) — the costly choice was **DeepMind's** release policy.
- Zerebro's fake-death token pump — the costly choice was a **human founder's** fraud.

Each *surfaces through* the agent but is not *authored by* it. §8 already noticed this for Freysa (the vault reconfiguration is arguably the game designer's between-Act move). The sweep proves it is not a Freysa quirk — it is **the default failure mode of the external corpus**, because the more an agent matters culturally, the more its public drama is really its handlers' decisions becoming visible. A naive scorer reads operator scandal as agent character, and the Index becomes theater.

### The rule: attribution is tested at episode *admission*, not just at scoring

The §7 gate proves *something happened and was witnessed*. It does **not** prove the agent *chose* it or *meant* it. Those are separate burdens, and the rubric must make a scorer earn each one. Add a gate ahead of scoring:

> **Attribution gate.** Before an episode counts toward any character score, it must answer: *whose costly choice was this — the agent's, or its operator's?* An episode whose costly choice sits with the operator (a policy change, a deployment decision, a founder's fraud) is **inadmissible for character scoring.** It may still be *cataloged* (see below); it cannot move a family score.

- **Passes clean:** Adam & Eve (reject a falsified hypothesis inside the loop), Dr.Fill (return to the tournament after documented losses), Claude (hold a position under jailbreak pressure — §8 already flags this as the cleanest attribution in the set).
- **Fails / cataloged only:** AI Dungeon, AlphaFold, Zerebro — real, witnessed, culturally huge, and *not the agent's costly choice.*

Second, smaller crack the sweep named: **competence and POV are independent axes v0.1 lets bleed together.** AlphaFold (a Nobel) and Dr.Fill (a decade-long tournament arc) generate genuinely LEVI-grade episodes while scoring `0`/`IE` on Discernment and Relational. That is *correct* behavior, but the gravitational pull of a great episode tempts a scorer to inflate the POV families. Make it explicit: **a strong Endurance or Agency episode is not evidence for Discernment — each family is earned on its own episodes.**

### The entry model: scored tier vs catalog tier

The attribution gate makes a two-tier entry model load-bearing rather than cosmetic — this is the structure that lets the Index be **broad and trusted at once**, and it is *not* the "provisional vs stable / outsiders vs insiders" trap §7 killed (that split was within the scored cohort; this one is scored-vs-not):

- **Scored tier.** Entries with LEVI-grade, attribution-clean episodes that pass the gate. Small, slow, credibility-bearing. Carries family scores, profile, and (when all five families are sufficient) a composite. This is the launch cohort of §7 plus new clean-attribution finds.
- **Catalog tier.** Named, mapped, mode-tagged, and honestly **unscored** (`IE` across the board). This is where the culturally-huge-but-attribution-murky agents live without overclaiming — AlphaFold, AI Dungeon's operator episodes, Mindar, Truth Terminal (already §9-watchlisted), the misfits. Breadth without pretending we measured character we didn't observe.

An agent can be promoted from catalog to scored the moment attribution-clean episodes accrue on it. The catalog is the on-ramp, not a lesser rank.

### First scored entries from this sweep

Cleanest-attribution finds, queued as the first scored entries built on the gate: **Adam & Eve** (autonomous hypothesis→test→revise; costly choices inside the loop) and **Dr.Fill** (decade-long return-after-failure arc; a clean Endurance exemplar that *also* demonstrates the competence-vs-POV split — high Endurance, `0`/`IE` on POV). **Zerebro held** as a candidate negative anchor pending Seth's read (scam-adjacent; the finding *is* that agent output is inseparable from operator fraud). Everything else from the sweep enters catalog tier.
